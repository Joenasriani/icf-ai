## Evidence basis

Evidence basis is multi-select. It records what kind of support exists; it is not itself the current verdict on the claim.

**DOC — Primary documentation**  
A primary documentation source states or specifies the relevant feature.

**EXP — Experimental / quasi-experimental**  
Controlled or quasi-experimental evidence addresses the causal contrast.

**OBS — Independent observation**  
Independent measurement repeatedly observes the phenomenon.

**COR — Correlational**  
An observational association is present but causal attribution remains unresolved.

**SIM — Simulation / model-based**  
A result is generated by a stated model or simulation and inherits that model's assumptions.

**NONE — No usable evidence basis**  
No usable evidence basis is currently available.

## Current claim status

Record one current assessment separately from evidence basis.

- **SUPPORTED** — Current evidence supports the claim within stated boundary conditions.
- **MIXED** — Evidence supports some conditions or components and conflicts with others.
- **NOT SUPPORTED** — Current evidence does not justify the claim.
- **CONTRADICTED** — Current evidence directly conflicts with the claim under the tested conditions.
- **UNRESOLVED** — Evidence is insufficient or identification remains uncertain.
- **HYPOTHESIS** — Testable claim proposed before adequate evaluation.

## Observability state

Record each major variable as:

- **DIRECTLY MEASURABLE** — observable or instrumentable directly.
- **INFERABLE** — estimable only through a specified measurement model or controlled tests; record the inference and assumptions.
- **INACCESSIBLE / UNKNOWN** — available evidence does not support reliable observation.

## Identification rule

A research design must state the assumptions that make its causal contrast identifiable, the evidence supporting those assumptions, plausible violations, and the conclusion that remains permissible if identification fails.

Counterfactual simulation is a model-based analysis tool; by itself it does not identify a real-world causal effect from observed data.
