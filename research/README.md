# ICF-AI — Integrative Causal Framework for AI Systems

**Public research record · Version 1.0 · 22 September 2026**

ICF-AI is a **provisional, modular causal research framework** for studying how AI systems are formed, deployed, experienced, acted through, and recursively changed by interaction with humans, organizations, markets, institutions, and environments.

It is intended for research questions where an observed AI behavior cannot responsibly be attributed to a single model, prompt, training method, interface feature, or institution.

## Core causal spine

1. **Conditions** — infrastructure, markets, law, standards, dependencies, institutional constraints.
2. **Formation** — data, annotation, architecture, pretraining, post-training, objectives, evaluation feedback.
3. **Deployment Stack** — specifications, instructions, memory, retrieval, tools, routing, security, moderation.
4. **Observable Interaction** — content, style, timing, tool use, refusal, uncertainty, action initiation.
5. **Human or System Response** — perception, cognition, affect, epistemic judgment, behavior.
6. **Action and Outcome** — digital or physical action, institutional decisions, resource allocation, state change.
7. **Collective and Institutional Effects** — teams, organizations, collective reasoning, coordination, decision rights.
8. **Feedback and Recursion** — new data, incentives, norms, regulation, adaptation, future AI systems.

## Research workflow

**Observed phenomenon → relevant causal layers → variables → causal DAG → estimand → identification strategy → evidence classification → model update**

The working rule is:

> Do not infer the causal mechanism from surface behavior alone.

## Cross-cutting controls

- Observability: directly measurable, inferable, inaccessible/unknown.
- Boundary conditions and version metadata.
- Time-scale separation.
- Intervention and counterfactual analysis.
- Latent-variable preservation.
- Nonlinearity and feedback stability.
- Measurement validity, reliability and replication.

## Evidence classes

- **A — Directly documented**
- **B — Experimentally demonstrated**
- **C — Independently observed**
- **D — Correlational**
- **E — Hypothesis**
- **F — Unknown**

Plausible mechanisms are valid research objects at **E**; they are not silently promoted to established facts.
