What it is

ICF-AI is designed for research questions where an observed AI behavior cannot responsibly be attributed to a single model, prompt, training method, interface feature, or institution. It provides a structured way to move from an observation to a causal research problem without pretending that the visible behavior already reveals its cause.

Start with the observation

Describe what happened in measurable terms before assigning a mechanism. This prevents the explanation from being smuggled into the question.

Separate the causal layers

Distinguish what came from model formation, runtime configuration, interface behavior, human response, institutional context, or later feedback.

Turn explanation into a test

Specify treatments, outcomes, mediators, confounders, estimands, assumptions, counterfactuals, and the evidence needed to change the claim status.

Preserve what is not known

Unknown telemetry, inaccessible system instructions, weak identification, and competing explanations remain explicit instead of being converted into confident narrative.

The 8-stage causal spine

  1. 01ConditionsWhat external environment made the phenomenon possible or constrained it?Compute, infrastructure, markets, law, standards, dependencies, institutional rules.
  2. 02FormationWhat processes shaped the capabilities or behavioral tendencies before deployment?Data provenance, annotation, architecture, pretraining, post-training, objectives, reward structures, evaluation feedback.
  3. 03Deployment StackWhat happened around the trained model at runtime?System specifications, instructions, memory, retrieval, tools, routing, safety controls, inference settings, privacy and moderation.
  4. 04Observable InteractionWhat can actually be observed at the interface or through instrumentation?Content, style, latency, tool use, memory use, citations, uncertainty, refusals and action initiation.
  5. 05Human or System ResponseHow did the recipient react, interpret, trust, verify, or act?Perception, cognition, affect, epistemic judgment, scrutiny, cognitive load, verification and behavior.
  6. 06Action and OutcomeWhat state change occurred after the interaction?Digital or physical action, collaboration, institutional decisions, resource allocation, task success, failure or other measurable outcome.
  7. 07Collective and Institutional EffectsWhat changes when the unit of analysis is a team, organization or distributed decision system?Collective intelligence, shared memory, coordination, attention, decision rights, correlated error and institutional resilience.
  8. 08Feedback and RecursionHow does the outcome become part of the next causal environment?User adaptation, new data, synthetic feedback, commercial optimization, regulation, norms, labor, markets and later systems.

OPERATIONAL METHOD

How a question moves through ICF-AI

The framework is not a checklist to fill mechanically. It is a sequence for turning a vague explanation into a bounded research design.

  1. Define the phenomenon

    Write the observation without causal language. “Sponsored recommendations appeared faster” is an observation; “the system prioritized advertisers” is already an explanation.

  2. Locate candidate causal layers

    Activate only the parts of the framework that could plausibly contribute. Unrelated categories remain inactive unless new evidence makes them relevant.

  3. Specify the causal question

    Define the treatment or exposure, comparator, outcome, mediators, moderators and candidate confounders. The goal is to say exactly what difference is being explained.

  4. Build the causal representation

    Use a DAG for an acyclic causal question. If genuine reciprocal feedback exists, represent it across time or with another suitable dynamic model rather than drawing an invalid cycle inside a DAG.

  5. Define the estimand

    State the numerical causal quantity you actually want to know. This forces the analysis to distinguish a broad topic from a measurable causal contrast.

  6. Justify identification

    Choose a design and record the assumptions required for the causal effect to be identifiable, the evidence supporting those assumptions, plausible violations, and the weaker conclusion that remains if identification fails.

  7. Record evidence on separate dimensions

    Evidence basis, current claim status, observability, uncertainty and replication are not collapsed into one score. A documented feature is not automatically a demonstrated causal effect.

  8. Update rather than defend

    Results may support, weaken, split, contradict or reframe a mechanism. Competing explanations and prior versions remain traceable.

CROSS-CUTTING CONTROLS

What the framework forces you to keep visible

Observability

Separate what can be measured directly from what can only be inferred and what remains inaccessible or unknown.

Boundary conditions

State where a result is expected to hold: model, version, task, population, account tier, region, time period or other limiting condition.

Time scale

Do not mix milliseconds of interface latency with weeks of user adaptation or years of institutional change as though they are one process.

Intervention and counterfactual

Ask what could be changed, what comparison would isolate the effect, and what outcome would be expected under the alternative condition.

Latent variables

Keep unobserved but plausible causes in the model instead of silently assuming they are absent.

Nonlinearity and feedback

Effects may depend on thresholds, interactions, saturation, adaptation or recursive loops rather than a simple linear chain.

Measurement validity

Define how a construct becomes an observable measure and whether the instrument actually captures what the study claims it captures.

Version and replication

AI systems change. Every claim must retain model/version/date metadata and distinguish first observation from replicated evidence.

Research status and scope

ICF-AI is presented as a provisional, versioned integrative causal research architecture, not as a universal theory of AI. Plausible mechanisms are treated as hypotheses until supported by evidence. The framework is descriptive before normative and is intended to be revised when evidence requires it.

The public GitHub record preserves the core structure, selected methodological documentation and version history so the framework can be inspected before purchase. The paid release packages the complete reader-facing research document together with the private AI-ready project-source layer.

What it does claimA structured research architecture for decomposing multi-layer AI phenomena and making causal assumptions, evidence and uncertainty explicit.

What it does not claimIt is not a universal theory of AI, not a peer-reviewed validation of itself, and not proof that any proposed mechanism exists on a particular platform.

How it should changeThe framework is versioned. Categories, relationships and records are expected to be revised when empirical evidence or methodological criticism requires it.
