ICF-AI

Integrative Causal Framework for AI Systems

A provisional, modular research architecture for tracing an AI phenomenon from system conditions and model formation through deployment, observable interaction, human or system response, real-world outcomes, and recursive feedback.

What it is

ICF-AI is designed for research questions where an observed AI behavior cannot responsibly be attributed to a single model, prompt, training method, interface feature, or institution. It provides a structured way to decompose the system, state the causal claim, define measurable variables, test competing explanations, and preserve uncertainty where evidence is incomplete.

The 8-stage causal spine

  1. ConditionsInfrastructure, markets, law, standards, dependencies and institutional constraints.
  2. FormationData, annotation, architecture, pretraining, post-training, objectives and evaluation feedback.
  3. Deployment StackSpecifications, instructions, memory, retrieval, tools, routing, security and moderation.
  4. Observable InteractionContent, style, timing, tool use, uncertainty, refusal and action initiation.
  5. Human or System ResponsePerception, cognition, affect, epistemic judgment and behavior.
  6. Action and OutcomeDigital or physical action, institutional decisions, resource allocation and state change.
  7. Collective and Institutional EffectsTeams, organizations, collective reasoning, coordination and decision rights.
  8. Feedback and RecursionNew data, incentives, norms, regulation, user adaptation and future AI systems.

How it is used

  1. Define the observable phenomenon without assigning a cause.
  2. Locate only the causal layers relevant to the question.
  3. Define treatment, outcome, mediators, confounders and moderators.
  4. Build a causal DAG and state the estimand.
  5. Select an identification strategy.
  6. Measure, classify the evidence and record boundary conditions.
  7. Update the causal model when evidence changes.

Cross-cutting controls

  • Observability: measurable, inferable, inaccessible.
  • Boundary conditions and version metadata.
  • Time-scale separation.
  • Intervention and counterfactual analysis.
  • Latent-variable preservation.
  • Nonlinearity and feedback stability.
  • Measurement validity and replication.

Research status and scope

ICF-AI is presented as a provisional, versioned integrative causal research architecture, not as a universal theory of AI. Plausible mechanisms are treated as hypotheses until supported by evidence. The framework is descriptive before normative and is intended to be revised when evidence requires it.

The public GitHub record preserves the core structure, version history and additive research rule. The paid Reference Edition packages the framework into a compact reader document with the operational research structure and reusable schemas.
